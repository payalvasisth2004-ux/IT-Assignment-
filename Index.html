Question 1 Bloom's Taxonomy: Remembering (Knowledge)

Computer Characteristics & Hardware Generations

Part A: Four Primary Characteristics of Computers

1. High Speed

Computers operate at ultra-high frequencies measured in Gigahertz (GHz), capable of executing millions to billions of instructions per second with zero fatigue or loss of accuracy.

2. Accuracy & Precision

Computers deliver 100% precision when provided with correct logic and instructions. Errors typically stem from flawed programming (Garbage In, Garbage Out - GIGO), not hardware failure.

3. Vast Storage Capacity

Massive volumes of data and instruction sets can be stored in primary memory and secondary storage devices (SSD/HDD), retrievable instantaneously.

4. Versatility & Diligence

Capable of handling diverse tasks ranging from complex mathematical modeling to multimedia playback and scientific simulations continuously without getting tired.

Part B: Hardware Identification Across 5 Generations

Gen 1 (1940-56)

Vacuum Tubes

Bulky, consumed massive power, generated extreme heat. Example: ENIAC, UNIVAC.

A rigorous analytical comparison of low-level programming languages (Machine Code & Assembly) versus high-level programming languages (C++, Java, Python) across execution speed, hardware dependence, translation requirements, and programmer complexity.

Parameter Low-Level Languages (Machine/Assembly) High-Level Languages (Python/Java/C++)
Execution Speed Extremely Fast (Direct hardware execution) Slightly slower (Requires compilation/interpretation layer)

Question 5 Bloom's Taxonomy: Analyzing (Analysis)

Input Precision: Point-and-Draw Devices vs. Graphics Digitizers

An in-depth analysis of operational mechanisms comparing standard point-and-draw devices (Optical Mice & Trackballs) with professional Graphics Digitizers and Drawing Tablets tailored for CAD modeling and digital art.

Optical Mouse & Trackball

General / Standard CAD

Uses LED/laser sensors and DSP to track relative movement across flat surfaces, or stationary trackballs where the user rotates a ball with fingers.

  • Excellent for general OS navigation and web browsing
  • Ergonomic variants reduce wrist strain
  • Lacks pressure sensitivity for organic digital sculpting

Graphics Digitizer / Drawing Tablet

Professional CAD / Art

Electromagnetic or capacitive sensor grid mapping absolute coordinates on a tablet surface paired with a stylus offering 8,192+ levels of pressure sensitivity.

  • Absolute coordinate mapping for exact scaling
  • Pressure and tilt sensitivity for natural brush strokes
  • Steeper learning curve and higher hardware cost
Question 6 Bloom's Taxonomy: Evaluating (Evaluation)

Technical Trade-offs: Plotters vs. UHD Monitor + Large-Format Printer

An evaluation of output systems for a Civil Engineering and Architectural firm requiring high-precision blueprint drafting. We compare traditional vector plotters against a modern setup combining ultra-high-definition (UHD) monitors and large-format inkjet printers.

Option A: Traditional Vector Plotter

Directly draws lines on paper using mechanical pens guided by digital vectors.

Unsurpassed crisp line accuracy for CAD blueprints

Highly cost-effective for continuous monochrome line drawings

Inefficient for photorealistic renderings and solid shading

Option B: UHD Monitor + Large-Format Printer

Interactive high-resolution digital canvas paired with high-dpi inkjet printing for full-color plans.

Exceptional flexibility for 3D modeling and color schematics

Allows collaborative digital markup before physical printing

Higher ongoing ink/maintenance consumable costs

Professional Recommendation by Payal Sharma (1826071)

For modern civil engineering workflows, Option B (UHD Monitor + Large-Format Inkjet Printer) is recommended. While vector plotters excel in pure line precision, modern architectural firms require photorealistic rendering, topographical color coding, and digital collaboration that only a digital workstation ecosystem provides.

Question 7 Bloom's Taxonomy: Evaluating (Evaluation)

Critical Analysis: Vision-Input vs. Voice Recognition in Adverse Environments

Critical evaluation of sensory input devices under adverse conditions: Vision-input (facial recognition / gesture cameras) versus Voice recognition in high-ambient-noise, publicly exposed environments.

Vision-Input (Facial Recognition / Cameras)

Relies on optical sensors and computer vision algorithms to identify users or track gestures.

Silent operation (unaffected by ambient acoustic noise)

Highly vulnerable to lighting variations, shadows, and occlusions (masks/glasses)

Privacy concerns and susceptibility to spoofing via high-res photos

Voice Recognition Systems

Relies on acoustic microphones and natural language processing (NLP) neural models.

Works in complete darkness or obstructed camera angles

Severely degraded accuracy in high-ambient-noise public stations (trains, streets)

Lack of acoustic privacy in public spaces (commands easily overheard)
